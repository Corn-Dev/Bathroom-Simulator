# Short Form Core Guidance

Stable guidance for short form videos (Reels, TikToks, YouTube Shorts).
This file is author-controlled and changes rarely.
Performance-based insights live separately in `short-form-insights.md`.

---

## The Fundamentals

**The first 2–3 seconds must give the viewer a reason to stay.**
Don't bury the interesting thing. Open with the payoff, the broken thing, the surprising moment. The hook is not an intro — it's a reason to not scroll.

**One idea per short.**
Don't try to cover multiple features or topics. Pick the single most interesting thing and go deep on that one thing. If you find yourself writing "and also..." — cut it.

**The visual should carry the story.**
If you removed the voiceover, the viewer should still roughly understand what's happening. Gameplay and screen recordings do the heavy lifting. Text overlays can reinforce, not replace.

**End with a payoff or a hard cut.**
Don't trail off. Either land on something satisfying (it works, the bug is fixed, the reveal) or cut hard right after the peak moment. Fading out or rambling after the point is made kills the video.

**Under 60 seconds unless the content earns more.**
Most ideas don't need more than 45 seconds. If you're going over 60, ask whether you're covering one idea or two.

**Personality matters more than production quality.**
A genuine reaction, a funny moment, a real struggle — these outperform polished but flat content at this stage. Don't over-produce.

---

## Hook Patterns That Work

**The broken thing** — open on the bug, the glitch, the thing that looks wrong. Viewer wants to know what happened.

**The reveal** — open on the finished result. "I built X" with the thing on screen. Viewer wants to know how.

**The question** — pose a problem the viewer can relate to. "What happens when two players try to fish the same spot?" Viewer wants the answer.

**The before** — show the bad version first. Makes the after land harder.

---

## What to Avoid

- Opening with your face talking before showing anything interesting
- Spending more than 3 seconds on setup before the hook
- Covering two separate ideas in one short — pick one
- Ending with "anyway that's it" or trailing off after the point
- Text overlays that just repeat what you're saying — use them to add, not echo
- Over-explaining the technical side — show the effect, not the cause

---

## Voice and Tone

Short form is even more casual than long form. You have 60 seconds — every word counts. Cut filler. Get to the point. Let the gameplay do the talking where possible.

The viewer is a gamer. They don't need to understand how it works — they need to feel why it's cool, funny, or interesting.
